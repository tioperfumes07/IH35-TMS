THE ATTRIBUTION METHOD — owner ruling 2026-09-03
======================================================================
Use the HIGHEST RUNG a cost can reach. Never skip to allocation because
it is easier.

RUNG 1 — DIRECT TRACE. Use for almost everything.
  Fuel, tolls, scales, repairs, lumper, permits.
  Each was incurred by ONE truck at ONE place on ONE date. Nothing to
  split. A diesel purchase on 08/07 at Palestine AR belongs to whichever
  truck was in Palestine AR on 08/07 — that is a FACT, not an allocation.
  This is also the only version that survives an audit or an IFTA
  review. An allocated fuel cost cannot; a traced one is a receipt.

RUNG 2 — TRACE TO THE LEG; the leg carries the truck.
  Driver pay and revenue. Each leg has its own linehaul and its own
  miles, so both attribute by leg with no judgment.
  If T156 ran the NB and T163 ran the SB, the NB's revenue and driver
  pay belong to T156 and the SB's to T163. BOTH TRUCKS PARTIALLY
  ASSIGNED — and it comes out of the data, not out of a percentage.

RUNG 3 — ALLOCATE. Only for costs that genuinely belong to the whole
  trip. There are very few. Basis is MILES, because miles are what the
  cost actually varies with in trucking.
  FIXED MONTHLY COSTS — insurance, plates, the truck note — DO NOT
  BELONG ON A TRIP AT ALL. They are PERIOD costs on the UNIT. Forcing
  them into a settlement makes trip margin meaningless.

THE TEST FOR ANY ATTRIBUTION RULE:
  Could you point a CPA at a document, or only at a formula you chose?
  POINT AT A DOCUMENT.
