PART 2 — TWO CORRECTIONS I OWE. BOTH MINE.
================================================================================
CORRECTION 1 — "THE INVOICE USES PRACTICAL MILES" WAS WRONG.
  GO-16 REV-A said practical miles are what we bill on. They are not. The owner
  was explicit and Cursor was right to catch it:

    THE CUSTOMER PAYS THE TYPED RATE. Destination plus rate is the booking.
    Practical miles exist only to compute REVENUE PER MILE, so the lane can be
    judged. Revenue per mile = line haul divided by PRACTICAL miles.
    Short miles pay the driver. Empty miles live inside short.

  Nothing about the invoice is computed from a mile count. Any seat that wires
  billing to a mileage figure is building the wrong company's software.

CORRECTION 2 — I OVERSTATED THE TEAM DEFECT.
  I wrote that the settlement engine may be broken until this block lands. It is
  not, and I did not verify before saying it. Live pay resolves through
  `mdata.driver_teams`. The table I named, `team_settlement_splits`, is not in
  this repo at all.

  THE REAL P0 IS NARROWER AND STILL REAL:
    Never rebuild lane statistics from Driver Pay divided by Driver R.P.M.
    without the team flag. On a team load the rate is per driver and the pay is
    the combined total, so the division doubles the miles. That is how Phenix
    City to Laredo read 4,433 miles on a 1,358-mile trip in my first seed.
  It is a rule about deriving statistics. It is not an accusation against the
  settlement engine.

PART 4 — BUILD. CURSOR'S ARCHITECTURE, WHICH IS BETTER THAN MINE.
================================================================================
DO NOT ADD A SECOND PAIR OF MILEAGE COLUMNS.
  The load already carries `miles_practical` and `miles_shortest`. Book Load
  already writes them, pay already reads `miles_shortest`, and `loaded_miles`
  copies from shortest. A parallel pair is a dual write path and a future
  reconciliation problem. Reuse what exists.

  ADD ONLY:
    mdata.loads.mileage_source          text
    mdata.loads.mileage_variance_flag   boolean default false
    mdata.loads.stop_count              text
    mdata.load_stops.postal_code        text
  Map the seed's names to the existing columns in the API layer, never in the
  table.

NEW TABLE — catalogs.lane_mileage, as specified in REV-A section 2, with these
changes:
    confidence          text  CHECK IN ('High','Check ZIP','Thin','Manual')
    autofill_allowed    boolean NOT NULL DEFAULT false
    source              text  CHECK IN ('History','Manual','Routing engine')
  Values are stored in the English the operator reads. No tokens.
  FORCED row level security, both partial unique indexes, verified with
  pg_index — pg_constraint returns a false empty on partial indexes.

AUTO-FILL RULE — the single most important line in this packet:
    FILL ONLY WHERE autofill_allowed IS TRUE. 474 lanes.
    Everything else shows the hint and leaves the boxes EMPTY.
  Filling 1,353.7 for Chicago as though it were a surveyed number, when those
  123 runs span 351 miles, teaches the dispatcher to trust a figure that is not
  trustworthy. A typed value always wins and marks the field as operator entered.

EXCLUDED FROM LOOKUP ENTIRELY:
    same origin and destination city, and any lane with three or more runs and a
    spread over 100 miles — 112 of them. They would look precise and be wrong.

RESOLUTION ORDER, each labelled in plain English on screen:
    ZIP match ......... "Matched by ZIP"
    City match ........ "34 prior runs, median 456.7 miles"
    Reverse lane ...... "From the reverse lane, 17 prior runs"
    No match .......... "New lane. Enter the miles."

THE LEARNING LOOP — unchanged from REV-A and still the point of the build. Every
delivered single-stop load updates its lane. Multi-stop loads are skipped. A
lane entered by hand becomes Thin at one run and High at three inside a 20-mile
spread. Nobody maintains a spreadsheet.

MILES STRIP LABELS — fix in the same pull request:
    Practical miles ... used for revenue per mile
    Short miles ....... what the driver is paid on, deadhead included
    Empty miles ....... deadhead to the pickup
    Revenue per mile .. line haul divided by practical miles
  Delete the old "fuel and ETA" wording and delete the "PC*MILER is not
  connected" note. Neither is true and both teach the wrong model.

PC*MILER — do not wait for it. History first is correct for this company because
the historical figures ARE the medians of what we actually ran. When it is
connected later it arrives as source 'Routing engine', is compared against the
lane median, and never silently replaces a number a customer already agreed.
Version-lock the engine year at that point; contracts pin the version.
