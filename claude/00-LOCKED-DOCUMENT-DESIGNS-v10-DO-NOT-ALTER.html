Downtime, idle and lost opportunity

Three tiers, never mixed. Cash margin is what hit the bank. True-cost margin swaps fuel purchased for fuel consumed. Economic margin subtracts what the week actually cost you in lost time, idle diesel, repositioning and revenue that never happened. Lost revenue is real money you did not earn — but it is not a cash expense and it never posts to the ledger. It lives on the settlement as management information, so the books stay clean and you still see the truth.

How the downtime engine works

1 · Every stop the truck makes that is not a pickup, a delivery or a legal break opens a downtime event. It carries a start, an end, a location, a category, a reason and a fault. Start and end come from Samsara; the reason and the fault are entered by a human, and the event cannot be closed without both.

2 · Category and fault are catalogs, not free text — otherwise nothing is ever reportable. Category: breakdown waiting · breakdown in shop · between loads · detention at shipper · detention at receiver · border wait · weather hold · HOS reset · no load available · driver personal. Fault: ours-dispatch · ours-driver · ours-maintenance · customer · shipper · receiver · vendor-parts · road-service · weather · border · undetermined. "Undetermined" is allowed, and it ages — an event still undetermined after 7 days goes on an exception list.

3 · If the event runs past one calendar day, a reason is mandatory for each day it continues. Exactly your case: Saturday to Monday is one reason — road service was not contracted — and Monday to Tuesday is a different one — the alternator had not arrived. Two days, two reasons, two different parties at fault. One lump of "downtime" would have hidden both.

4 · Idle fuel is measured, not assumed, and only when the engine was running. The breakdown window burned nothing because the engine was dead — the ledger above shows 73.7 hours against 0.000 gallons, and that is the correct answer, not an oversight. For real idling, the hours come from Samsara's engine-on idle, never from elapsed clock time.

5 · On the idle burn rate — 1.0 gal/h is high; I checked. The U.S. Department of Energy puts an idling vehicle at about 0.8 gal/h, and an unloaded semi at about 0.64 gal/h; a loaded truck, or one running HVAC hard in Laredo in August, burns more. Argonne's own idle-consumption test data works out near 0.45 gal/h for a bare idle with no accessory load. So the honest default is 0.80 gal/h, not 1.00 — and even that is only a default. Samsara reports idle fuel per unit directly, so the engine uses the unit's own measured rate when it is available and falls back to 0.80 with the method recorded on the line. Same discipline as mpg: the number is never printed without saying where it came from.

6 · A cancelled load keeps its full record. Load number, customer, PU date, pickup and delivery locations, miles and the amount that would have been invoiced — all of it, plus the cancellation timestamp, the reason and the fault. It shows on the settlement as lost opportunity. It is never invoiced, never accrued and never journalised.

7 · Repositioning miles are lost miles and are costed as such. When the replacement load picks up in a different city, the miles and the diesel to get there belong to the event that caused them — here, the cancellation — not to the new load. Otherwise the replacement load carries a cost it did not create and its margin lies.

Real fuel cost per load · the engine

The problem, in your words: a driver tops off 80 miles out of Laredo at the end of one load, so that load eats a full tank and shows a crushed margin, while the next load runs on borrowed diesel and shows an inflated one. Assigning fuel to the load that bought it is a cash fact, not a cost fact. The tank is inventory, and inventory is costed when it is burned, not when it is filled.

How the engine works

1 · The tank is inventory, costed at weighted average. Every unit carries gallons on hand and a value on hand. A fill adds gallons and dollars and re-averages. A load burns gallons at the average in force at that moment. This is standard average-cost inventory — the same method NetSuite uses for stock — so it stands up to a CPA without an argument.

2 · Gallons burned come from driven miles, not practical miles. Gallons = driven miles ÷ mpg. Driven miles come from the odometer, which is why the odometer column exists. You said it plainly: a driver may run 1,500 miles on a load paid at 1,200 because he routed around a DOT scale. The customer is billed practical, the driver is paid short, and the diesel is burned on driven. Three different numbers, three different jobs, never conflated.

3 · MPG is measured, never assumed, and the method is recorded. In order: tank-to-tank when two consecutive fills are both fill-to-full and both have an odometer (miles between ÷ gallons of the second fill) · else the unit's rolling 90-day actual · else the fleet average for that unit class. Every computed fuel cost records which method produced it, so no number on a report is ever unattributable.

4 · The Loves geofences already do the hard part. The unit enters a fuel geofence, the app records date, time and odometer from Samsara. When the fuel expense is later created for that unit on that date, the app suggests that odometer. A suggestion, shown with its source and its timestamp, that a human accepts or overrides — never a silent auto-write.

5 · Cash and cost stay separate, so nothing breaks. You are on cash basis, so the books and the bank keep using fuel purchased — that is what left the account and that is what ties to AlwaysTrack and to the statement. Fuel consumed is a management number for load profitability and cost per mile. The settlement shows both lines, side by side, and the difference is simply the change in the tank. Nothing is restated and nothing stops tying out.

6 · Missing data is shown as missing. No odometer at load start or end means that load's consumed cost is marked estimated, with the method named, and the per-driven-mile figures print —. If the tank ever computes negative gallons on hand, that is a flagged exception — a missed purchase or a wrong mpg — not something quietly clamped to zero.

Invoice generation and factoring submission

1 · The invoice is generated by the load closing, not by a person remembering. When a load reaches delivered and its documents are present, the invoice is minted from the rate confirmation — customer, terms, PU number, PO, BOL, appointment windows, contacts and the billed practical miles all come from that document, not from retyping.

2 · The document set is the gate. Rate confirmation, BOL and POD. The driver's photo upload through the app IS the BOL — when it is already in docs.files and linked to the load, the invoice generates with it attached. If a required document is missing the invoice is created but held as not submittable, and the load board says which document is missing. It is never sent to the factor incomplete, because an incomplete package is how a purchase gets declined and the whole batch delayed.

3 · Submission to Faro is automatic once the package is complete — invoice PDF plus rate confirmation plus BOL plus POD, as one batch, with the submission timestamp and batch id written back onto the invoice. Live today: 95 invoices advanced ($335,591.72) and 58 not factored ($220,529.00).

4 · The assignment notice is not optional on a factored invoice. It names Faro as the only valid payee. Without it a customer can pay USMCA directly on a receivable that has already been sold, and then the money has to be chased and repaid.
